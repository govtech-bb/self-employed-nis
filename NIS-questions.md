# Questions for NIS (the MDA)

Organised so the questions that unblock the calculator come first. Drawn from the
"Questions for MDA" in the meeting notes, the gaps flagged in the contextual brief,
and the placeholder figures in `check.html`.

Each question has:
- **Plain:** the everyday way to ask it out loud in the meeting.
- **Refers to:** what the question is actually about.
- A **status** tag (see key).
- An **Answer:** line — pre-filled where a document already gives it; tick the box once NIS confirms.

**Status key:** ✅ answered in the documents (just confirm) · 🟡 partly answered, needs NIS to confirm the exact number/rule · 🔴 no answer anywhere, NIS must provide · ⚪ internal decision (not an NIS question)

## 1. Benefit amounts — the calculator can't go live without these (the main blocker)

- [ ] ✅ **1. Sickness benefit formula.** At the $1,200/year minimum, is the weekly sickness benefit **~$30** or **~$133**?
  - Plain: If someone pays the lowest amount ($1,200 a year), how much do they actually get a week if they're off sick? We've seen two numbers — about $30 and about $133 — which is right?
  - Refers to: how much a person gets per week if they're off sick, at the lowest contribution level. This is the single number blocking the calculator.
  - Answer (nis.gov.bb/sickness-benefits): **Sickness Benefit = 66⅔% of average insurable weekly earnings, ÷ 6 for the daily rate**, paid per workday excluding Sundays. At the $1,200/yr minimum (~$46/wk insurable) → 66⅔% of ~$46 ≈ **~$30/week**, confirming the lower figure; the $133 reflected a higher contribution level. First 3 days are unpaid "waiting days" (paid if illness lasts 2+ weeks). Payable up to **26 weeks**, extended to **52 weeks** with 150+ contribution weeks. Self-employed need 7 contributions in the relevant quarter, 13 weeks insured, and 39+ contributions over four consecutive quarters. *Confirm the minimum-level worked example.*
- [ ] ✅ **2.** What **% of insurable income** does each benefit replace, and is there a floor and ceiling?
  - Plain: For each benefit, how much of a person's normal income does it replace? Is there a smallest and largest amount?
  - Refers to: the replacement rate — e.g. sickness pays X% of your insurable earnings, up to a max.
  - Answer (NIS benefit pages): replacement rates differ by benefit — **Sickness 66⅔%**, **Maternity 100%**, **Paternity 100%**, **Invalidity Pension 40%** of average annual insurable earnings (+1% of insurable earnings beyond the first 500 contributions, capped at **60%**). Floors/ceilings: Invalidity Pension minimum **$254.00/week**; the insurable-earnings ceiling is **$1,201/week** (as at 1 Jan 2024). *Confirm the current ceiling and whether a sickness/maternity floor applies at the $1,200/yr minimum.*
- [ ] 🟡 **3. Maternity benefit** — total entitlement for a qualifying event.
  - Plain: Having a baby — how much does maternity pay, and for how many weeks?
  - Refers to: how much, lump sum and/or weekly, and for how many weeks, when someone has a baby.
  - Answer (nis.gov.bb/maternity-grant-benefits): **Maternity Benefit = 100% of average insurable weekly earnings, ÷ 6 for the daily rate** (NIS example: $3,000/month → $692.31/week, $115.38/day). Women who don't qualify for the benefit instead receive the **Child Grant lump sum of $1,280** (child must be born in Barbados). Self-employed need 39+ contributions over four consecutive quarters, plus 16 contributions in the two quarters but one before. *The page does not state the number of weeks payable — confirm the maternity benefit duration.*
- [ ] ✅ **4. Paternity benefit** — amount and duration.
  - Plain: New dads — how much do they get, and for how long?
  - Refers to: paid time off / payment for new fathers.
  - Answer (nis.gov.bb/paternity-benefit): **Daily rate = 100% of average insurable weekly earnings ÷ 6.** Payable for **3 weeks** after the birth (can be split: at least 2 weeks within 3 months of birth, at least 1 week before the child turns 6 months). Only **one Paternity Benefit per 12-month period**. Applies to a child born **on or after 1 June 2025**. Self-employed need contributions totalling at least **$1,200 in the previous year**.
- [ ] ✅ **5. Invalidity benefit** — how is it calculated and what's the minimum?
  - Plain: If someone can't work long-term because of illness or injury, how is the amount worked out?
  - Refers to: ongoing payment if a long-term illness or injury stops someone working.
  - Answer (nis.gov.bb/invalidity-grant-benefit): two forms. **Invalidity Grant** (lump sum) = **6 weeks' average insurable weekly earnings per 50 contributions** paid/credited — needs 50+ contributions. **Invalidity Pension** (ongoing) = **40% of average annual insurable earnings + 1%** of insurable earnings beyond the first 500 contributions, **capped at 60%**, with a **minimum of $254.00/week** — needs 150+ contributions. Both require being under pensionable age and permanently incapable of work. Self-employed must have averaged 39+ contributions a year. The pension converts to the Old-Age Contributory Pension at pensionable age.
- [ ] ✅ **6. Survivors' benefit** — who qualifies (spouse, children) and how much?
  - Plain: If someone dies, who gets money — partner, children — and how much?
  - Refers to: what a person's family receives if they die.
  - Answer (nis.gov.bb/survivors-grant-pension): **Survivors' Benefit** is a grant or pension depending on the deceased's entitlement, payable if they were receiving or qualified for Invalidity or Old-Age Contributory Benefit. **Spouse** (incl. common-law; married 3+ years): aged **50+** gets **½** of the deceased's invalidity/old-age benefit for life; aged **45–49** gets **⅓** of the maximum pension; others get it for **one year**. **Each child** (unmarried, maintained by the parent) gets **⅙** of the parent's amount, or **⅓** if an orphan/invalid — payable to age **16**, or **25** if in full-time approved education. Total survivors' benefit can never exceed what the deceased would have received.
- [ ] ✅ **7. Funeral grant** — exact amount and who can claim it.
  - Plain: How much is the funeral grant, and who can claim it?
  - Refers to: the one-off payment toward funeral costs.
  - Answer (nis.gov.bb/funeral-grant): **The Funeral Grant is $2,220**, paid to whoever met (or is liable for) the funeral cost. Covers insured persons receiving or with title to Sickness, Maternity, Invalidity or Old-Age Contributory Benefit (and self-employed persons on the same basis), plus spouses of insured persons and deaths from employment injury. The amount is uniform regardless of contribution level.
- [ ] 🟡 **8. Old age pension** — confirm best-10-years basis and the minimum contributory pension floor.
  - Plain: Is the pension based on a person's best 10 years of earnings, and what's the smallest pension someone can get?
  - Refers to: the monthly income at retirement, and the smallest pension someone can get.
  - Answer: Brief confirms a **minimum contributory pension floor exists** (§2.2), but not the dollar amount or the best-10-years rule. Official name is the **Old-Age Contributory Pension** (the invalidity page confirms the invalidity pension converts to it at pensionable age). *Still need NIS to confirm the best-10-years basis and the dollar floor — not stated on the benefit pages reviewed.*

## 3. Back-pay, deadlines & penalties

- [ ] ✅ **14.** Confirm **back-pay up to 3 years** and the **5% surcharge** — per missed year, and on what base?
  - Plain: People can pay for up to 3 missed years — is that right? And the 5% late fee — is that per year, added on top each year?
  - Refers to: catching up on missed years and the late fee that applies.
  - Answer (from brief data model): **Up to 3 missed years, 5% surcharge per missed year.** *Confirm the calculation base.*
- [ ] ✅ **15. Annual contribution deadline.** Confirm the **15 January** cut-off.
  - Plain: Is the deadline to pay for last year really 15 January?
  - Refers to: the date a self-employed person's contributions for a year must be paid in by. This is what triggers the 5% surcharge (#14) if missed.
  - Answer (from brief §1.2): The minimum $1,200/yr is **payable by 15 January of the succeeding year**, as a single payment or instalments. (So 2026's contributions are due by 15 Jan 2027.) *Confirm it applies to all contributors with no sector exceptions.*
- [ ] 🟡 **16.** Any penalty for a **missed or skipped month**?
  - Plain: If someone skips a month, is there any penalty? We're telling people there isn't — is that correct?
  - Refers to: whether someone is penalised for not paying in a given month (we tell users there's no penalty).
  - Answer: The voluntary/instalment model implies no per-month penalty (surcharge applies to missed *years*), but it's not stated outright.
